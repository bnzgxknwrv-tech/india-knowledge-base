### 3. Ghantewala — a 1790 Delhi sweet-shop lineage that died and came back

- **Place/region:** Chandni Chowk, Old Delhi
- **Category:** resurrected cult institution; living commercial-history oddity
- **What the traveler actually does/sees/experiences:** Visit the reopened Ghantewala at its historic Chandni Chowk address and try the lineage-defining Sohan Halwa or another old-style sweet while looking at how an institution with Mughal-era roots has been rebuilt for the present.
- **Why exceptional:** This is not simply “an old sweet shop.” Founded in 1790, it shut in 2015 after more than two centuries and reopened in August 2024 under the same family lineage. The unusual experience is the rupture-and-revival story: a supposedly vanished Delhi institution physically back in Chandni Chowk.
- **Traveler signal / repeated praise:** Reopening reporting records immediate old-customer and walk-in interest; later Delhi heritage writing treats the shop’s return as part of Chandni Chowk’s living rather than museum-like continuity.
- **Nearest current cluster/corridor:** Delhi / Old Delhi corridor
- **Approximate detour / extra time class:** ~5–15 min if already on the central Chandni Chowk spine; ~15–30 min tasting/inspection stop.
- **Could plausibly add cluster time?** **NO**.
- **Visitability / seasonality caveat:** **Important atmosphere caveat:** the current outlet is a **2024 modern/swanky re-creation**, not an untouched 1790 interior. Its value is lineage and comeback, not preserved furniture. Reconfirm opening shortly before travel.

### 4. Indian Coffee House, Mohan Singh Place — worker-cooperative time capsule above Connaught Place

- **Place/region:** Mohan Singh Place, Baba Kharak Singh Marg / Connaught Place, Delhi
- **Category:** social-history cult café; fading institution; human atmosphere
- **What the traveler actually does/sees/experiences:** Take the lift/stairs into the dated Mohan Singh Place building and sit in the Indian Coffee House for coffee/snack amid old canteen furniture, long-time patrons and the residual atmosphere of Delhi’s cooperative coffee-house culture.
- **Why exceptional:** Delhi’s Indian Coffee House was not just a café but an intellectual/political meeting room. The earlier Connaught Place operation was demolished during the Emergency era; the cooperative’s Mohan Singh Place premises have been in use since 1969. The surviving place is valuable precisely because it feels unfashionable and resistant to normal café redevelopment.
- **Traveler signal / repeated praise:** Travel writing repeatedly calls it a nostalgia/time-capsule experience; recent personal travel writing still describes entering the building as “walking back in time.” Current business/traveler listings confirm the Mohan Singh Place operation remains visitable, though overall food/service ratings are modest.
- **Nearest current cluster/corridor:** Delhi / central Delhi corridor
- **Approximate detour / extra time class:** ~5–15 min local deviation if already in Connaught Place; ~30–60 min stop.
- **Could plausibly add cluster time?** **NO**.
- **Visitability / seasonality caveat:** Go for the institution/room, not for a best-coffee claim. Current third-party listings disagree on hours, so verify same-day. The historical chronology of the old CP site versus the Mohan Singh premises is complex; the key physical fact is that the cooperative has occupied Mohan Singh Place since 1969.
