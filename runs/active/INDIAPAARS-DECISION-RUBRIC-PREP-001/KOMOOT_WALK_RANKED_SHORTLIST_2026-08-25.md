## C. CURRENT DOMINANCE / ANTI-MEDIOCRITY FINDINGS

### Finding 1 — Dhokaney establishes the short-route benchmark
Any future 3–5 h viewpoint candidate should have to beat Dhokaney's structural efficiency: only 30–45 min for a priority water/waterfall payoff. A long route with scenic payoff <4/5 or scenic density <4/5 is automatically suppressed under the rubric, regardless of kilometres, elevation or Komoot popularity.

### Finding 2 — Sattal must be route-compared, not area-scored
`Sattal / Seven Lakes` is an area/experience candidate, not yet a closed walk. The wrong response would be to take the longest Komoot loop simply because it reaches more lakes. The correct response is to compare variants and identify the smallest route that preserves the strongest lake/forest sequence, unless a longer route has sustained exceptional density.

### Finding 3 — Naini Lake cannot inherit A+ as a walk score
Nainital's existing A+ is not evidence that the full 3.2 km lake loop is the best scenic walk. The loop must independently prove scenic density, daypart value and low road/crowd dilution. A shorter shore segment may dominate the full loop if it preserves most of the beauty with less exposure; alternatively the full loop may win if its continuity is itself the experience. Evidence decides.
