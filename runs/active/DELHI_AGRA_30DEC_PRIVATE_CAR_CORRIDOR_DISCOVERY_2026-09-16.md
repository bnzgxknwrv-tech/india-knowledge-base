## C. NEW CANDIDATE FOUND

### Nidhivan, Vrindavan — direct Sri Ramakrishna visit site (1868)

- **Full recognition-rich name**: Nidhivan (Nidhuvan) — a sacred grove/temple complex in central Vrindavan, one of the town's most visited sites generally, but carrying a specific, previously-unsurfaced Ramakrishna layer.
- **Physical place**: a walled sacred forest/garden with a temple complex (Rangaji-adjacent area of central Vrindavan), open daily, entry closes by evening (~7–8pm) per local tradition; a major, easy, fully paved-access pilgrimage site, not a remote or hard-to-reach location.
- **Person/event**: Sri Ramakrishna visited Vrindavan at age 32 in late February 1868, lodging nearby at Fouzdar Kunj (Retia Bazar) arranged by Mathur Babu. From there he visited Nidhivan, where he **met Gangamata**, an elderly Vaishnav sadhika living there, and had a vision associated with **Vankavihari** (a form of Krishna). Ramakrishna's love for Vraja/Vrindavan during this pilgrimage was so intense that he reportedly considered staying permanently, only returning to Kolkata because of his mother.
- **Evidence class**: EXACT / primary — sourced to Swami Saradananda's authoritative Ramakrishna biography (already in this repo's own TOP-11 Ramakrishna reconciliation: `runs/active/TOP11-RAMANA-RAMAKRISHNA-V2-PRE-EXTERNAL-001/RAMAKRISHNA_V2_PRE_EXTERNAL_FREEZE.md`, row 16, "The Master visited places like Nidhuvan in Vrindavan... vision of Vankavihari... Gangamata of Nidhuvan"), independently corroborated this session via fresh web search against Ramakrishna Mission's own Vrindavan Sevashrama historical pages and independent Vraja-focused sources, which agree on the same 1868 visit, same companions (Mathur Babu, Hridayram), and the Nidhivan/Gangamata detail.
- **Current visitability**: fully open, high-traffic pilgrimage/tourist site, no access restriction beyond normal daily hours (~5am–7/8pm).
- **Secondary, lower-confidence detail**: **Fouzdar Kunj, Retia Bazar** — the exact building where Ramakrishna lodged during this visit — still exists and is named in Ramakrishna Mission's own historical account, but is described as privately maintained by the "Mukherji brothers," not an official pilgrimage site; current public access is **unverified** and should not be presented as a confirmed visitable stop, only as a "still stands, access unclear" footnote if this ever reaches Mark.
- **Why this plausibly reaches Mark-A caliber**: Ramakrishna is explicitly on Mark's high-priority person list; this is a real personal-presence/event site (not a generic Krishna-devotional temple visited by "everyone"), backed by primary biographical text, and was missed by every earlier Vrindavan pass in this repo because those passes were built entirely around the Yogananda/AOAY layer and the Neem Karoli Baba layer — the same two-research-modes-never-cross-reference pattern documented in this session's earlier Kakrighat semantic-losslessness audit (`runs/active/GLOBAL_PLACE_PERSON_SEMANTIC_LOSSLESSNESS_2026-09-16/`). The Ramakrishna TOP-11 person sweep had this row all along; it was simply never checked against the Vrindavan place file.
- **Corridor geometry / net detour — corrected 2026-09-21 (Mark asked for real numbers, first pass was too casual)**: **ON-CORRIDOR, small not zero.** Nidhivan sits ~1 km from Vrindavan railway station and ~1 km/~700 m from Banke Bihari Temple — genuinely walkable together with **Katyayani Peeth**, which independent sources also place ~1 km from the same railway station/bus stand (so Nidhivan↔Katyayani Peeth is realistically a short walk or a couple of minutes by auto-rickshaw). **Neem Karoli Baba Vrindavan Ashram** is a real town-crossing further out: it sits on Parikrama Marg near Mathura Road, independently reported as ~5–8 km from the Banke Bihari/Nidhivan area (two sources disagree, 5 km vs. 7.9 km) — roughly ~15–25 min by auto-rickshaw/car in local Vrindavan traffic, not a "few minutes." SMALL DETOUR bucket for that leg specifically, not ON-CORRIDOR/near-free as first stated.
