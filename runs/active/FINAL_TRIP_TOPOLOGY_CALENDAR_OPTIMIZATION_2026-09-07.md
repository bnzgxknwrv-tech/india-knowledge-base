## 3. Plausible macrospines tested

### 1. Current clean eastbound spine — RECOMMENDED

`Delhi -> Kumaon -> Agra -> Bodh Gaya -> Varanasi/Sarnath -> Kolkata/Dakshineswar -> Tiruvannamalai -> Delhi`

Strengths: all locked durations fit; the three overnight trains convert necessary distance into sleep; Bodh Gaya–Varanasi uses a short daytime rail product; Varanasi–Kolkata and Kolkata–Chennai have direct-air service patterns; Kolkata is before the Gangasagar peak and Tiruvannamalai retains four complete Pongal-period days. Weaknesses: 30 December requires a Delhi station transfer after an overnight train; 31 December Taj/12988 is fog- and holiday-exposed; the 19 January spare is best spent as Chennai positioning rather than left free.

### 2. Agra before Kumaon — REJECTED / DOMINATED

This would put a second intercity movement after AI156, hotel recovery and Nirmal Dham on 19 December, or force Taj Mahal on 20 December followed by a Delhi-crossing journey back toward Kumaon. It worsens jetlag arrival quality, duplicates the Delhi corridor, and either shifts the locked first leg or consumes the only spare. Avoiding 31 December Taj crowds is not enough to compensate.

### 3. Kolkata / Varanasi reversal — SERIOUS ALTERNATE, BUT LOSES

`...Agra -> Bodh Gaya -> Kolkata -> Varanasi/Sarnath -> Tiruvannamalai -> Delhi`

It can place Kolkata around 3–5 January and avoids Gangasagar build-up. However, the current direct Gaya–Kolkata flight pattern is thin and afternoon-weighted; rail options impose very early Gaya boarding. It then needs another flight back to Varanasi and a less frequent Varanasi–Chennai nonstop/connection. The clean order instead uses daily-pattern 20887 Gaya–Varanasi on Sunday 3 January and the dense Kolkata–Chennai corridor. No Kalpataru benefit is gained because locked Bodh Gaya still occupies 1 January.

This becomes preferable only if (a) Mark's later Kolkata grading requires avoiding all Gangasagar build-up, and (b) bookable, humane direct Gaya–Kolkata, Kolkata–Varanasi and Varanasi–Chennai flights are simultaneously proven on the resulting dates.

### 4. Tiruvannamalai / Kolkata reversal — REJECTED

`...Varanasi/Sarnath -> Tiruvannamalai -> Kolkata -> Delhi`

It can still touch Pongal, and ending in Kolkata gives a dense Delhi air corridor. But it places Kolkata roughly 16–18 January, closer to Gangasagar peak/return flows; requires the less dense Varanasi–Chennai edge plus a second south-to-east flight; and breaks the clean north/east-to-south progression. It wins no night.

## 4. Kumaon internal order

### Recommended

`Nainital/Hotel Evelyn -> Kainchi Dham + Bhumiadhar as a Nainital day -> Dunagiri Retreat/Kukuchina/Mahavatar Babaji's Cave -> Haidakhan Vishwa Mahadham -> Haldwani/Kathgodam rail gateway`

Why it survives independent testing:

- It places three decompression sleeps before the highland/Kriya segment and six acclimatisation sleeps before the rule-bound ashram.
- Kainchi/Bhumiadhar is naturally reachable from Nainital without a luggage move.
- Dunagiri to true Haidakhan is road-only but can produce a humane 13:00–14:30 arrival from an 08:30–09:00 departure.
- Ending at Haidakhan puts the last mountain module closer to the plains rail gateway than ending at Dunagiri, despite unresolved exact entrance kilometres.
- The psychological progression is lake recovery -> Maharajji pilgrimage -> Babaji/Kriya highlands -> two complete days inside Haidakhan ashram rhythm.

### Serious alternative checked

`Nainital -> Haidakhan Vishwa Mahadham -> Kainchi Dham/Bhumiadhar -> Dunagiri Retreat/Kukuchina/Mahavatar Babaji's Cave -> rail gateway`

This may reduce one internal road segment under some map routings, but it moves the heaviest adaptation world forward and ends deep in the eastern highlands, requiring a long descent before the night train. It also risks turning Kainchi into a luggage-transfer stop. Exact road data are too conflicted to claim a kilometre winner; whole-human geometry clearly favours the recommended order.

## 6. Weather/season execution consequences

- Delhi, Agra, Gaya and Varanasi sit inside the north-Indian winter-fog risk system. IMD documented dense/very dense fog in Delhi and Uttar Pradesh in the comparable December period: <https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20251215_pr_4559.pdf>. Therefore the 30 December Delhi rail bridge must have a fallback and the 31 December 12988 must not be followed by a brittle same-morning appointment.
- Kumaon roads should be daylight-led. The schedule gives the two longest mountain moves morning departures and does not require a same-day rail connection until 20:52 on 29 December.
- Kolkata in January is operationally milder, but Gangasagar creates human congestion independent of weather.
- Chennai/Tiruvannamalai weather is usually less disruptive than northern fog; Pongal demand, not climate, is the binding concern. The 175 km airport road is still a 3.5–4.5-hour whole-human transfer, not a raw map-time promise.

### Failure tests

- One missed 30 December onward connection: use a later Delhi–Agra train or road fallback and retain the Agra night; only a major delay threatens the Taj morning.
- One-night 12988/fog disruption: the base's movable Jan19 slot can be released upstream, but Chennai positioning is then lost. The trip still fits; final-flight robustness becomes weaker.
- Kolkata 4 nights or Bodh Gaya 3 nights: either consumes the single slot. Both together fail by one night.
- Delhi arrival 19 January: objectively safest for AI155 because it adds a second Delhi hotel night; it requires using the one spare in Delhi and doing Tiruvannamalai–Chennai road plus MAA–DEL on 19 January. That is a longer, more failure-coupled day and contradicts Mark's current 20 January preference.
- Delhi arrival 20 January: recommended only with a separate Chennai positioning sleep on 19 January and a morning nonstop MAA–DEL on 20 January. It gives roughly a one-day international buffer but not two.
