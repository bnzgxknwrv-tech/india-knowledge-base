## Scoring rule

This heatmap does not produce a destination choice. It exposes independent dimensions so Mark can later decide with minimal hidden assumptions.

- `PERSON_COUNT`: conservative `MIN_CONFIRMED_COUNT` from `ALL_PERSON_CITY_OVERLAP.md`.
- `EXACT_SITE_COUNT`: number of already identified strict shared physical sites/complexes in or directly assigned to the place/cluster. Event-zones count separately and are labelled.
- `PHYSICAL_DENSITY`: qualitative concentration of multiple meaningful physical sites inside one city/compact project cluster: `VERY_HIGH / HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / SINGLE_ANCHOR`.
- `EVIDENCE_CONFIDENCE`: confidence in the currently used overlap layer, not saturation of the whole person corpus.
- `CONFLICT_LOAD`: `LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH` based on unresolved identity/chronology/personal-presence warnings relevant to travel interpretation.
- `LOCKED_ANCHOR_STATUS`: preserves Mark locks; a lock is not a heatmap score.
- `LOGISTICS_CONCENTRATION_POTENTIAL`: only whether existing known sites are concentrated enough to plausibly combine within one travel base/area. No route, time, nights or hotel assumptions are made.
- `MARK_DECISION_REQUIRED`: `NEE` only where Mark already locked the destination-level anchor or where the item is purely subordinate/contextual; otherwise `JA`.

## Anti-ranking guardrail

Do **not** sort the final trip solely by `PERSON_COUNT`. That would incorrectly penalize Arunachala and the Babaji cave, both of which have explicit project/Mark importance independent of cross-person overlap. Likewise, do not let a large metropolitan aggregation such as Kolkata masquerade as a single compact site. The final human decision should compare at least: mission anchors, person overlap, exact-site quality, physical density, evidence confidence, and unresolved gaps.
