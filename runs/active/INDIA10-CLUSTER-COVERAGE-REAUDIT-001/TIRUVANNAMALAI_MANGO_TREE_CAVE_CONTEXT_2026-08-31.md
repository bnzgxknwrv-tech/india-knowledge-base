## IDENTITY / NAMES
- Mango Tree Cave.
- Also encountered as **Mamara Guhai** / **Chuta-guha** in Ramana literature.
- Do not confuse it with the earlier **mango orchard / Mango Grove near Gurumurtam** where young Ramana stayed for a period in 1898. This is a different place.

## HISTORICAL VALUE
During Ramana Maharshi's long Virupaksha Cave period (1899–1916), Virupaksha could become oppressively hot and its water supply poor in summer. Ramana used Mango Tree Cave as a cooler summer residence because the nearby **Mulaipal Tirtham** provided a reliable supply of water.

The cave is therefore not merely a named hole in the hill: it belongs directly to Ramana's cave-life period and to the practical rhythm of that life on Arunachala.

Ganapati Muni also lived at Mango Tree Cave. Ramana-related archival material records the famous **Uma Sahasram** episode here: Ganapati Muni composed the final portion of the thousand verses in Ramana's presence.

## GEOGRAPHY / ROUTE RELATION
Multiple Ramana/Arunachala sources describe Mango Tree Cave as:
- on the **eastern slope of Arunachala**;
- **below Virupaksha Cave**;
- near **Mulaipal Tirtham**;
- on the **common/main way up to Virupaksha Cave from the town / Arunachaleswarar Temple side**.

This matters for the current Day-4 direction:
`Sri Ramanasramam rear -> Skandashram -> Virupaksha Cave -> town-side descent`.

Because Mango Tree Cave lies below Virupaksha on the common town-side approach, the best current planning hypothesis is that it should be a **natural stop on the descent after Virupaksha**, rather than a separate out-and-back or vehicle excursion.
