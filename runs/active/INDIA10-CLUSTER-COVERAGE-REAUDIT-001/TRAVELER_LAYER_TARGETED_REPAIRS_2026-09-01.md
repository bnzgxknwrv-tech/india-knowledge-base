Official shop history: established **1911** on Tekari Road; traditional hand-pounded sesame+jaggery/sugar tilkut. It also makes anarsa, lai and peda. This strengthens the existing Gaya Tilkut A* by giving it a concrete institution and human story.

### Anarsa — Gaya/Magadh fresh sweet
Lonely Planet writer Pratishtha Singh explicitly singled out returning to Gaya for fresh anarsa because it loses quality in transport. This is a good example of food as place-specific travel content, not generic restaurant filler.

### Bodh Gaya's winter international-food microculture — IMPORTANT NEW TRAVELER LAYER
Multiple traveler/community sources show that Bodh Gaya's pilgrim season creates an unusually international small-town food scene: Tibetan, Thai, Bhutanese, Burmese/Vietnamese/Japanese-influenced food around the Kalachakra/Be Happy/Fujiya area. A Jan-2026 Reddit report specifically describes a **winter-only street-food lane near Be Happy Cafe**, with Thai/Burmese/Bhutanese/Vietnamese vendors and laphing; a 2025 high-engagement IndiaFood/Bihar community discussion names places such as Tashi, Kunga and Pralom. Current Tripadvisor 2026 lists Tibet Om Cafe and notes Oct–Mar seasonality.

This is a genuine previously under-preserved traveler finding: **Bodh Gaya in December/January has an international winter pilgrim-food village atmosphere.** It is perfectly seasonal for this trip and can be enjoyed as food/evening texture without creating a new day.
