## FINDING 1 — ANCIENT STUPA RIDGE ABOVE THE CAVES
**Working label:** Ancient Pragbodhi/Dungeshwari stupa ridge — ruins of seven early Buddhist stupas on the ridge immediately above the ascetic caves, with sweeping valley views.

### Why this is important
A 2025 archaeological field survey by Dr. Amit Ranjan (Bihar Heritage Development Society / Indian Journal of Archaeology) documented seven stupa structures along the ridge above the cave plus monastic/temple remains lower on the hill. The paper explicitly states that there is no regular way to the peak and records exact survey coordinates/elevations.

Independent pilgrimage sources also describe the rough path above the cave, surviving ancient stupa remains and broad views; one notes that the Mahabodhi spire can be seen in the distance.

This is not another car stop. It is a same-hill extension of the cave visit.

### Geometry from the archaeological survey
Cave survey point: `24.73664, 85.04753`, elevation ~200.9 m MSL.

Selected stupa survey points:
- Stupa 1: `24.73623, 85.04730`, ~237.5 m MSL — ~51 m straight-line from cave / ~37 m higher.
- Stupa 3: `24.73669, 85.04836`, ~257.6 m MSL — ~84 m straight-line / ~57 m higher.
- Stupa 4: `24.73812, 85.04932`, ~288.5 m MSL — ~244 m straight-line / ~88 m higher.
- Stupa 5: `24.73929, 85.05021`, ~308.2 m MSL — ~400 m straight-line / ~107 m higher.
- Stupas 6–7 extend to ~440 m straight-line from cave / ~108 m higher.

Important: these are horizontal/straight-line comparisons from surveyed coordinates, NOT a claim about actual walking distance. The survey says there is no regular way to the peak; ridge edges are steep/sloping and full circumambulation is not possible at several stupas.
