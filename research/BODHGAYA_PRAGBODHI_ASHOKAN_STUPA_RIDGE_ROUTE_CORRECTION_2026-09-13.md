## Core correction

A022 must not be presented as merely `ancient stupa remains above the cave`.

The stronger meaning is:
- Xuanzang records that King Ashoka erected markers/stupas on Pragbodhi to commemorate the Bodhisattva's ascent/descent on the mountain;
- recent archaeological fieldwork documented seven substantial ancient stupa remains distributed along the ridge, plus monastic/temple remains lower on the hill;
- the ridge therefore forms a materially important pilgrimage landscape tied to the traditional movement of Siddhartha from the austerity mountain toward Bodh Gaya.

## Archaeological layout

2025 field survey (`Archaeology at Pragbodhi Mountain / Dungeshwari Hill`, Amit Ranjan, Indian Journal of Archaeology) records:
- cave around 24.73664, 85.04753;
- seven stupas distributed on the NE-SW ridge;
- Stupa 1 at the southwest end, directly above/south of the cave;
- Stupas 3-7 progressively farther northeast along the ridge;
- a monastic/temple structural area lower on the hill near the approach to the cave;
- no regular maintained route to the ridge peak; terrain is steep/rough.

The paper's discussion of Xuanzang says Ashoka built monuments/stupas at the places where the Bodhisattva ascended and descended the mountain.

## Important routing consequence

The seven stupas are NOT laid out as a simple one-way line that naturally continues downhill toward Sujata/Bodh Gaya.

They extend along the ridge, with most of the documented remains northeast of the cave, while Sujata/Bodh Gaya lie southwest.

Therefore a pilgrim who wants to see all seven would likely need to:
1. visit Dungeshwari/Mahakala cave;
2. climb to the ridge;
3. traverse the stupa chain along the ridge;
4. return/continue toward the southwest side of the mountain;
5. descend toward the plain;
6. then follow the known pilgrimage/countryside route toward Sujata and Bodh Gaya.

A direct continuous `all seven stupas -> obvious marked descent -> Sujata` route has NOT yet been established operationally in the project.

This is not a reason to devalue the route. It means the correct execution task is to find a local guide/monk familiar with the Ashokan stupa ridge and southwest descent so that Mark can combine the ridge pilgrimage with the walk toward Sujata/Bodhi Tree without unnecessary backtracking.

## Known continuation toward Bodh Gaya

Pilgrimage guides describe a dirt/countryside route from the Dungeshwari/Pragbodhi area along the base of the hills toward the Falgu/Nairanjana river and Bodh Gaya. This is the natural continuation after descending from the ridge.

Recommended Mark-facing experience:

**Taxi to Dungeshwari -> cave -> Ashokan stupa ridge / A022 -> southwest descent with local guide -> countryside Buddha-footsteps walk -> Sujata Mandir / offering-place -> Sujata Stupa -> river -> Bodhi Tree / Mahabodhi.**

This should be treated as one coherent Buddha-life pilgrimage day, not as disconnected checklist stops.
