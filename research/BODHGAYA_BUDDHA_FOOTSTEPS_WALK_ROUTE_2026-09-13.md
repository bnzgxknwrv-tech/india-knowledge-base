# BODH GAYA — BUDDHA FOOTSTEPS WALK: DUNGESHWARI -> SUJATA -> BODHI TREE

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: VERIFIED CONCEPT / LIVE ROUTE VERIFICATION STILL REQUIRED
Branch: agent/india8-cluster-casting

## Bottom line

There is a known and repeatedly described pilgrimage walk connecting Pragbodhi / Dungeshwari with Bodh Gaya through the Neranjara/Falgu countryside. It is not just an INDIA19 invented route.

For Mark, the preferred direction is:

**taxi to Dungeshwari -> visit caves -> optional A022 ridge -> descend to base -> walk west/southwest through countryside -> Sujata Temple / banyan offering place -> Sujata Stupa/Garh -> cross/approach Neranjara/Falgu into Bodh Gaya -> Mahabodhi/Bodhi Tree -> hotel.**

This follows the traditional biographical direction of Siddhartha's movement from austerity toward nourishment/Middle Way and then enlightenment.

## Evidence that the walk is a known pilgrimage route

1. BuddhaNet Pilgrim's Guide to Buddhist India, Pragbodhi:
   - describes walking all the way from Bodh Gaya to Pragbodhi on foot;
   - says to head east from Bodh Gaya, cross the Neranjara and Mohana Rivers, then follow the mountains north to the cave;
   - calls it a pleasant walk of about 6 km through attractive countryside.
   - Reverse direction is usable for Mark's preferred pilgrimage order.

2. Nekhor / Sitavana-Pragbodhi guide:
   - explicitly says it is possible to trace the Buddha's footsteps from the austerity caves toward the Vajrasana/Bodhi Tree;
   - instructs pilgrims to locate the dirt path along the base of the Dungeshwari Hills and follow it to the Falgu/Nairanjana riverbed.

3. Bodhi Travel pilgrimage account:
   - describes a half-day walk through the nearly dry riverbed, mustard fields and rural landscape associated with the Buddha;
   - crosses into Bakraur/Sujata world.

4. Bihar Tourism / District Gaya:
   - Dungeshwari is officially presented as the pre-enlightenment austerity place before Bodh Gaya;
   - Sujata Village/Stupa lies across the Falgu from Bodh Gaya and is about 2 km from Mahabodhi according to District Gaya;
   - October-March is the recommended period for the local Buddhist circuit.

## Distance / effort

Verified coordinates in project registry:
- Dungeshwari cave area -> Sujata Stupa straight-line: ~6.2 km;
- Sujata Stupa -> Mahabodhi: ~1.0 km straight-line;
- Mahabodhi -> Maya Heritage: ~0.86 km straight-line.

Known pilgrimage-guide walking description gives ~6 km for Bodh Gaya <-> Pragbodhi, but modern field/village/river access can lengthen the practical route.

Planning class for Mark:
- expect roughly **8-11 km total walking** from Dungeshwari back to Mahabodhi/hotel depending on exact dirt path, river crossing, Sujata detours and whether A022 is included;
- about **2.5-3.5 h pure walking** at an unhurried pace;
- with caves, Sujata sites, pauses, photographs and contemplation: **4.5-6 h pilgrimage block**;
- A022 ridge adds separate climbing/time and should remain conditional.

This distance is comfortably walkable for a normal fit traveller; the key uncertainty is route-finding/river access, not total distance.

## Safety / navigation status

Do NOT call this an officially marked trail.

As of the 2026-09-13 research pass:
- no reliable official GPX/waymarked trail was found;
- the known route uses dirt paths, village tracks, fields and the riverbed/causeway environment;
- river crossing/access can change seasonally;
- January is normally dry and is within Bihar Tourism's October-March recommended visiting season, but local path/access must still be checked.

Recommended execution:
- use taxi/auto for one-way morning drop at Dungeshwari;
- ask a monk/local guide at Dungeshwari for the current footpath toward Bakraur/Sujata;
- preferably hire a local guide for the Dungeshwari -> Bakraur section if the track is not obvious;
- once at Bakraur/Sujata, the return to Bodh Gaya is straightforward and close to the urban/pilgrimage zone;
- carry offline map, water and daylight margin.

## Important Sujata correction / missed physical distinction

The project A020 currently names **Sujata Stupa / Sujata Garh**, but authoritative tourism material distinguishes another nearby place:

**Sujata Mandir / Sujata Temple — the traditional banyan-tree offering spot where Sujata is believed to have given milk-rice to Siddhartha.**

Incredible India states that Sujata Stupa is associated with Sujata's residence and that a roughly 10-minute walk leads to Sujata Mandir, the place believed to mark the actual offering under the banyan tree.

This is spiritually more directly tied to the event than the Stupa alone and must not disappear inside a generic `Sujata Stupa` card.

Current status:

- Sujata Mandir / banyan offering place is a **separate physical candidate discovered/recovered by research**;
