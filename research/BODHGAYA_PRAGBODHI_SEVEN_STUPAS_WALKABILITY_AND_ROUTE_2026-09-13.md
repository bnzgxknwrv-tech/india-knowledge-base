## Core finding

The seven ancient stupa remains form a compact chain along the steep northeast-southwest Pragbodhi/Dungeshwari ridge above the cave, not a multi-kilometre trail.

The 2025 archaeological survey records the cave at about 200.9 m MSL and the highest of the documented stupas at about 309.2 m MSL. The chain itself is roughly 0.4-0.5 km one way along the ridge, with approximate spacings of 30-40 m, 100 m, 90-100 m, 100 m, 40 m and 40 m. From the cave to Stupa 7 the straight geographic displacement is about 0.44 km, with roughly 108 m net elevation gain; actual walking distance is longer because of terrain.

Recommended combined ridge budget: **~1.5-2.5 hours** from cave and back to the safe descent point.

## Safety / onward route

The archaeology paper explicitly states there is **no regular way to reach the peak from the cave**. Several stupas stand on sloping/steep edges and circumambulation is impossible at multiple remains because of the terrain.

North of Stupa 7 is a flat courtyard-like space followed by a **steep valley descending to the northeast**. This is the wrong direction for Sujata/Bodh Gaya and should not be treated as the onward walking route.

Default practical plan:
`taxi -> Dungeshwari cave -> climb to Stupa 1 -> ridge to Stupa 7 -> retrace southwest along ridge -> descend via known cave/visitor side -> countryside pilgrimage walk toward Sujata -> Bodh Gaya.`

Do not plan a direct descent from Stupa 7 toward Sujata unless a competent local guide confirms a safe current route on the day. A local southwest cut may exist, but current evidence is not strong enough to make it the itinerary default.

## Buddha-life meaning

Xuanzang's account describes Siddhartha approaching Pragbodhi from the northeast, ascending the northeast slope to the summit, then descending the southwest side and stopping at the cave. The 2025 archaeological survey explicitly connects the Ashokan monuments/stupas with the ascent/descent places preserved in that tradition.

For Mark-facing presentation, the ridge is therefore not generic archaeology. It is the ancient pilgrimage-marking layer of Siddhartha's movement over Pragbodhi before the transition toward the Bodh Gaya/Uruvela world.
