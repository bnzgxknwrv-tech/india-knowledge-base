## Bottom line

Mark is NOT inventing a new pilgrimage route.

There is a documented recurring official **Gyan Yatra / Buddha Marg** from Dungeshwari/Pragbodhi to Bodh Gaya, organised by the Gaya district administration, Bihar tourism authorities and the Bodh Gaya Temple Management Committee. Recent editions in 2025 and 2026 had roughly 1,000 participants and ran from near Mahakala/Dungeshwari Cave via Sujata Garh to Mahabodhi. Reported event distance varies by route/year around 8–9 km.

Separately, BuddhaNet and Incredible India explicitly describe climbing from Dungeshwari Cave to the ancient stupa remains on the summit/ridge. A 2025 archaeological survey mapped **seven** surviving stupa structures on the ridge.

The strongest executable version for Mark is therefore:

**taxi to Dungeshwari -> cave -> ridge out-and-back seeing all 7 stupas -> return to cave/foothill -> join Gyan Yatra/Buddha Marg alignment southwest -> Sujata milk-offering spot [A+] -> Sujata Stupa/Garh [A] -> cross river corridor -> Mahabodhi/Bodhi Tree -> hotel.**

## Ridge coordinates from 2025 archaeology survey

Cave: 24.73664 N, 85.04753 E, ~200.9 m MSL.

- Stupa 1: 24.73623, 85.04730, ~237.5 m
- Stupa 2: survey text says 30–40 m NE of Stupa 1. The publication repeats the Stupa 1 coordinate; treat that numeric duplication as a publication/data issue and identify S2 in the field from the ridge sequence.
- Stupa 3: 24.73669, 85.04836, ~257.6 m
- Stupa 4: 24.73812, 85.04932, ~288.5 m
- Stupa 5: 24.73929, 85.05021, ~308.2 m
- Stupa 6: 24.73968, 85.05005, ~308.4 m
- Stupa 7: 24.73985, 85.05011, ~309.2 m

Cave to Stupa 7 is only about 442 m straight-line, with about +108 m elevation. The real foot distance is longer because the ridge is steep/irregular and there is no regular maintained path to the summit.

The paper describes the stupas as a southwest-to-northeast chain. Stupa 1 is above the cave; S2 is 30–40 m NE of S1; S3 roughly 100 m farther; S4 and S5 farther along the same ridge; S6/S7 are clustered near the NE end. Several stupas cannot be circumambulated because their edges fall away steeply.

## Recommended ridge execution

Do the ridge as an **out-and-back**, not as a speculative descent beyond Stupa 7.

Reason:
- north of Stupa 7 the survey records a flat courtyard-like area, after which the terrain drops into a steep valley toward the northeast;
- northeast is the wrong direction for Sujata/Bodh Gaya;
- no source found documents a safe maintained descent from beyond S7 directly toward Sujata;
- returning along the known ridge to the cave/foothill avoids unnecessary routefinding risk.

Practical ridge timing for Mark:
- cave visit / sit: 30–45 min;
- cave -> S1/S2 -> S3 -> S4 -> S5 -> S6 -> S7: ~45–70 min at careful pace including stops;
- S7 -> cave/regular descent: ~30–50 min;
- total ridge add-on: **~1.5–2.25 h**.

A local monk/guide who knows the ridge is strongly preferred for identifying all seven remains, especially Stupa 2, because the survey itself says there is no regular route to the peak.

## Known onward pilgrimage route: Gyan Yatra / Buddha Marg

This route is repeatedly walked publicly and is not speculative.

Evidence:
- 2015: district administration + tourism + BTMC organised an 8 km Gyan Yatra from Pragbodhi caves to Mahabodhi; officials described it as Siddhartha's last journey before enlightenment and planned to develop it as Gyan Marg.
- 2016/2017: repeated event editions; reports call the section Buddha Marg / Gyan Yatra.
- 2023: about 1,000 participants walked from Pragbodhi caves to Sujatagarh.
- 2025: >1,000 participants walked Dhungeshwari -> Sujata Garh -> Mahabodhi, reported ~9 km.
- 2026: about 1,000 participants again walked an ~8 km Gyan Yatra from Dhungeshwari Hills to Mahabodhi, halting at Sujata Garh.

This is the route family Mark should follow after returning from the ridge.

## Safety / execution rule

- The **Gyan Yatra section is proven walkable as a public pilgrimage route** and has been walked by large groups including monks, tourists and schoolchildren.
- The **ridge section is the technical part**: short in distance but rough, steep and unmaintained.
- Do not descend blindly beyond Stupa 7.
- Use a local ridge guide/monk for all seven remains.
- In January dry-season conditions this is normally a rational day walk, but river/field access and ridge footing must be checked locally.
