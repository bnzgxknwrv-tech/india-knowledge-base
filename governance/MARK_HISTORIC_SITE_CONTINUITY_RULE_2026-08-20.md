# MARK HISTORIC SITE CONTINUITY RULE

Date: 2026-08-20
Status: ACTIVE

## Core rule
For Mark's travel decisions, continuity of the HISTORIC PLACE is more important than survival of the exact historic structure.

A location can be a strong A even when a house, room, temple building, hermitage structure, gate, platform or other built element has been rebuilt or altered, provided evidence establishes that the present visitable site is the same historic grounds / compound / hermitage / ashram / station site / garden / landscape where the relevant person-event occurred.

Do NOT downgrade a site merely because the present building fabric is newer.

## Distinguish these questions
1. EVENT CERTAINTY — did the person/event occur?
2. SITE CONTINUITY — are these the same physical grounds/place?
3. STRUCTURE CONTINUITY — is the building/room/gate/etc. itself still historic?
4. MICROPLACE CONTINUITY — can the exact room, tree, pond, steps, garden spot, platform, cave, gate, perron, etc. be identified?

These are separate fields. Failure of #3 or #4 must not erase a strong #1 + #2.

## Mark's travel meaning
If Yogananda and another person lived, walked, talked, meditated, sat, ate, slept or otherwise spent time throughout a hermitage/ashram compound, visiting those same grounds can retain high experiential value even if one or more structures have since been rebuilt.

Original fabric is a bonus, not a prerequisite.

## A-site end-stage requirement
For every Mark A site, the final travel guide must later attempt micro-resolution: original surviving structures, rooms, gardens, ponds, gates, steps, trees, caves, platforms, station/perron areas, photo positions, etc.; what happened at each; who was present; current access; and uncertainty. Never invent precision.

## Presentation rule
When presenting a candidate to Mark, explicitly state SITE CONTINUITY separately from BUILDING CONTINUITY. Avoid vague wording such as 'not the same building' when the historically important grounds are securely the same place.
