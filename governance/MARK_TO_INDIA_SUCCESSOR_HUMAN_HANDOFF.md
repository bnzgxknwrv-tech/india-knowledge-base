# 3. PHOTO / VISUAL RULE

When appearance is materially part of why a traveler would want to visit something, include representative photos when the interface supports it.

This especially applies to:
- visually distinctive architecture;
- strange/quirky attractions;
- landscape/nature;
- street art;
- unusual temples/buildings;
- viewpoints;
- places whose appeal is hard to understand from words alone.

Recent failure example:
`Lotus Temple` was described in text without showing that it is a striking white lotus-shaped building. Mark independently looked up the image and immediately understood why it was interesting. The presentation had therefore hidden decision-relevant information.

Photos are not decoration. When the visual form is part of the travel value, they are part of the decision surface.

Maps likewise belong in the decision surface when geography matters. A map never replaces textual km/time/context.

# 5. `JE BENT ER TOCH` IS CENTRAL TO MARK'S GRADING

A place does not have one intrinsic operational value independent of the route.

A mildly interesting traveler item can become A/A* territory for Mark if it is almost free because he is already nearby. Conversely, a more famous place can be unattractive if it adds two hours of Delhi traffic.

Therefore every burden-sensitive candidate must be tested from:
- actual hotel/base when known;
- otherwise an explicit transparent proxy;
- the nearest retained A+/A/B that Mark might visit anyway;
- the immediately preceding/following plausible stop;
- incoming/outgoing transfer corridor when relevant.

Show practical km + CONSERVATIVE human travel time.

Do NOT only say:
`Airport -> Lotus Temple 18 km`.

Also show, when relevant:
`Hauz Khas Village — café-/kunstwijk + ruïnes rond water [B] -> Lotus Temple — witte lotusbloemtempel [OPEN]: ~8 km / humanly 15–25 min`.

That is the information Mark uses to decide.

# 6. HUMAN TRAVEL TIME, NOT OPTIMISTIC MAP MINIMUM

If a route planner says 8 minutes in Delhi, do not build a decision surface around `8 min` as if it were a promise.

Use human planning ranges with traffic/access/buffer appropriate to the situation, e.g.:
- raw 8–10 min -> present perhaps 15–20 or 15–25 min;
- airport/city cross-Delhi -> conservative traffic range;
- mountain roads -> whole occupied human burden;
- flights/trains -> access, waiting, baggage and onward transfer included where decision-relevant.

Use 15/30-minute human-scale planning blocks. Exact minutes only when an external timetable/reservation fixes them.

On-site dwell is not total trip cost.

# 15. HUMAN PACE

Mark wants breathing room, but not empty days.

Normal local-day target when geography/energy permit:
- 2 meaningful blocks minimum;
- preferably 3 meaningful blocks;
- early experience can be followed by a 2–3 h recovery break;
- meaningful afternoon and often early-evening content can still follow;
- 4–5 stops are fine if they form one compact physical complex/walk.

Do not interpret “relaxed” as `one attraction then hotel all day`.

At the same time, do not make the final Delhi decompression day into a cross-city endurance test.
