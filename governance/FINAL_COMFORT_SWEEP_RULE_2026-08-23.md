# FINAL COMFORT SWEEP — HARD TRAVEL PHASE

Date: 2026-08-23
Hardened: 2026-08-29
Status: MANDATORY AFTER ROUTE / NIGHTS / DAY STRUCTURE ARE STABLE

## PURPOSE
After clusters, sleep bases, route order, nights, exact calendar and practical day structure are stable, run a separate COMFORT / FOOD / HUMAN-TEXTURE SWEEP per retained sleep base and actual day corridor.

This phase is intentionally deferred until geography is stable because the useful question is not “what are the best restaurants in this city?” but **“given where Mark actually sleeps / walks / finishes this day, where is the best realistic coffee, breakfast, lunch, dinner or memorable small stop nearby?”**

This phase must NOT be forgotten. A route is not final-travel-guide-ready until the sweep is complete for every retained base and relevant transfer corridor.

## HARD ANCHOR RULE — ACTUAL PLACE, NOT GENERIC CITY
Research from the actual chosen hotel/ashram and the actual day blocks/endpoints, not from a generic city centre.

For every candidate comfort stop show, where relevant:
- exact relation to the hotel / current day location / route;
- real walking or vehicle distance/time;
- daypart fit with the actual itinerary (including very early starts and late finishes);
- whether it is genuinely on the way or requires a detour;
- whether that detour is worth it.

## REQUIRED SEARCH CATEGORIES
Per sleep base / day area, research at minimum where relevant:
- **early breakfast** compatible with the planned start time;
- **best genuinely good coffee** / serious coffee addresses;
- **lunch** that fits the real midday geography rather than forcing a cross-city trip;
- **dinner** near the hotel or realistic end-of-day point;
- historic / cult pastry shops and bakeries;
- local signature sweets and regional specialties;
- strongest banketbakker / patisserie-type stops;
- one or two exceptionally characterful cafés / tea houses;
- deliberately good restaurants with strong local relevance and traveler consensus;
- old shops / markets / food institutions with real story or human texture;
- high-quality small food stops worth a short detour;
- practical comfort stops useful on long transfer days.

Where materially useful for a difficult day/base, also flag a nearby practical rescue option such as pharmacy, ATM/cash, laundry, bottled-water/snack shop or dependable rest/toilet stop. Do not clutter ordinary day cards with generic utilities.

## USER-PREFERENCE WEIGHTING
Mark is especially sensitive to:
- old/historic pastry shops and bakeries;
- local sweet specialties;
- characterful pastry/café/tea stops;
- genuinely good coffee;
- restaurants that are strong enough to justify being deliberately chosen rather than random convenience;
- pleasant food/rest stops that improve a transfer day without creating needless road friction.

A tiny exceptional pastry shop may matter more to the lived quality of the journey than a generic major attraction.

## QUALITY RULE
Do not produce generic “top 10 restaurants” lists.

Use a short supported shortlist based on current evidence such as:
- strong local reputation / longevity;
- specialist food writing;
- credible community/forum consensus;
- recent traveler signal;
- current map/review evidence for opening and practical usability.

For each recommendation explain WHY it beats random convenience.

## OUTPUT REQUIREMENT — COMPACT COMFORT CARD
For each retained sleep base / relevant day corridor, create a compact card containing as applicable:
- name;
- type: breakfast / coffee / lunch / dinner / bakery / sweet / tea / market / transfer comfort / practical rescue;
- exact area and which hotel/day block it serves;
- why special;
- what specifically to order / try;
- real distance/time and mode from the point Mark is actually at;
- opening/daypart fit;
- opening risk / reservation need / cash-only or access issue when relevant;
- detour cost and whether it is worth it;
- confidence / source notes.

The shortlist should normally be small: enough to give Mark a strong nearby choice, not another research catalogue.

## ROUTE-AUTHORITY BOUNDARY
The comfort layer enriches the settled trip; it does **not** silently become a new route-design layer.

A café, bakery, restaurant or food stop may be recommended as a small natural detour, but it may not independently force:
- a new sleep base;
- an extra hotel night;
- a major route bend;
- loss of protected A+/A content;
- a brittle day.

If something discovered here is genuinely extraordinary enough that INDIA believes Mark may want to change the settled route for it, present the full marginal burden and displacement and leave that subjective reopening to Mark.

## LIVE-FACT RULE
Food and comfort addresses are volatile. At the final comfort stage, recheck current opening days/hours, recent operating signal, reservation need and practical location. Do not rely on an old research hit merely because it remains in Git history.

## ORDER OF OPERATIONS
HEAVY A CLUSTERS -> ROUTE/NIGHTS -> CALENDAR/CLOSURES -> HOTELS/BASES -> DAY STRUCTURE -> **FINAL COMFORT / FOOD / HUMAN-TEXTURE SWEEP** -> FINAL DAY CARDS.

A final day card is incomplete if it gives sights and transfers but leaves Mark to improvise every coffee, breakfast, lunch, dinner and recovery stop in the field.
